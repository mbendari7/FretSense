## Read

A *chord* is a group of notes played together. The simplest full chord is the **triad**: three
notes, called the **root**, the **3rd** and the **5th**.

- The **root** names the chord. A C chord has C as its root.
- The **3rd** is a 3rd above the root. It decides whether the chord is major or minor.
- The **5th** is a perfect 5th above the root (7 half steps) in both major and minor triads.

### Major and minor

| Triad | Notes from the root | Half steps above the root | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major | root, major 3rd, perfect 5th | 0, 4, 7 | C major: C E G |
| Minor | root, minor 3rd, perfect 5th | 0, 3, 7 | A minor: A C E |

The only difference is the 3rd: one half step lower makes the chord minor. C major is C E G;
C minor is C Eb G.

In chord symbols, a letter on its own means major (C), and a small m means minor (Cm).

Another way to see it: a major triad is a major 3rd with a minor 3rd stacked on top (C to E, then
E to G). A minor triad is the other way round: a minor 3rd, then a major 3rd (A to C, then C to E).

### Chords from a key

If you build a triad on each note of the C major scale, using only notes from that scale, you get
the chords that belong to the key of C major:

| Degree | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chord | C | Dm | Em | F | G | Am | Bdim |

Bdim is a *diminished* triad: a minor 3rd and a diminished 5th (6 half steps). It is less common
in beginner songs, but now you know where it comes from. Many songs use only chords from this
list.

### Why it matters for your playing

Every major or minor chord shape you know is a root, 3rd and 5th, often with some of them doubled in other
octaves. Bassists outline chords by playing these tones. Guitarists use them to find new shapes
and to pick notes for fills.
